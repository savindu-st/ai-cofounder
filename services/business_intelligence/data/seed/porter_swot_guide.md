# Porter's Five Forces & SWOT Analysis Framework Guide

Strategic frameworks for early-stage startup viability assessment and competitive positioning.

---

## Part 1: Porter's Five Forces Framework

Michael Porter's Five Forces model assesses industry attractiveness, structural profitability, and competitive intensity.

### 1. Threat of New Entrants
- **Definition:** Ease with which new competitors can enter the market and erode incumbents' margins.
- **Barriers to Entry:**
  - Economies of scale (high upfront fixed capital requirements).
  - High switching costs for customers (integration lock-in, data gravity).
  - Intellectual property, proprietary algorithms, and regulatory moats.
  - Network effects (value increases as user base expands).
- **Startup Strategy:** Build defensible moats early via proprietary datasets, high switching barriers, or rapid distribution dominance.

### 2. Bargaining Power of Suppliers
- **Definition:** The leverage suppliers possess to raise prices or restrict supply quality.
- **Key Indicators:**
  - High concentration of upstream providers (e.g., reliance on a single foundational LLM provider or specialized GPU cloud).
  - Lack of viable substitutes for key inputs.
- **Startup Strategy:** Multi-vendor architecture (e.g., model-agnostic abstraction layers), open-source alternatives, and caching/optimizing core resource usage.

### 3. Bargaining Power of Buyers
- **Definition:** The power customers possess to exert downward pricing pressure or demand higher service levels.
- **Key Indicators:**
  - Low customer switching costs.
  - Undifferentiated, commoditized product offerings.
  - High buyer concentration (e.g., top 3 enterprise clients account for 80% of revenue).
- **Startup Strategy:** Deliver mission-critical ROI, personalize workflows, create high-utility pain relievers, and diversify target customer segments.

### 4. Threat of Substitute Products or Services
- **Definition:** Products or services outside the direct competitive set that solve the same underlying customer job-to-be-done.
- **Key Indicators:**
  - Existing manual workflows (e.g., Excel sheets, internal bespoke scripts).
  - Alternative emerging software modalities or consulting services.
- **Startup Strategy:** 10x improvement in cost, speed, or UX compared to legacy substitutes.

### 5. Competitive Rivalry Among Existing Competitors
- **Definition:** Intensity of competition among direct players in the space.
- **Key Indicators:**
  - Number and relative size of incumbents.
  - Industry growth rate (slow growth exacerbates zero-sum price wars).
  - High exit barriers.
- **Startup Strategy:** Focus on an underserved vertical niche (wedge strategy) before expanding horizontally.

## Part 2: SWOT Analysis Framework for Startups

A structured mechanism for evaluating internal competencies and external environmental vectors.

### 1. Strengths (Internal, Helpful)
- Core technical competencies and unique proprietary capabilities.
- Speed of iteration and agility compared to large legacy players.
- Deep domain expertise or proprietary industry access of founding team.
- Modern, clean architectural stack without legacy technical debt.

### 2. Weaknesses (Internal, Harmful)
- Limited runway and initial working capital.
- Low brand recognition, unproven trust in enterprise domains.
- Small engineering/sales capacity; dependency on key individuals.
- Absence of established channel partnerships or historical sales data.

### 3. Opportunities (External, Helpful)
- Structural market shifts (e.g., regulatory shifts, AI democratization).
- Rapidly expanding addressable market segments (high CAGR).
- Incumbents failing to adapt to modern developer/user experience standards.
- Collaborative ecosystem plays and platform integrations.

### 4. Threats (External, Harmful)
- Incumbent platforms building native copycat features into existing suites.
- Rapidly changing platform APIs, rate limits, or foundational pricing shifts.
- Macroeconomic contraction curbing enterprise SaaS budgets.
- Aggressive pricing competition from well-funded venture-backed rivals.
